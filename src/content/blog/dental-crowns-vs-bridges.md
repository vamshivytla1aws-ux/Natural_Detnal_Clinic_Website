# Dental Crowns vs Bridges: What's the Difference?

When you have a damaged or missing tooth, your dentist has several restorative options to bring back the function and appearance of your smile. Two of the most common and effective treatments are dental crowns and dental bridges. 

Because both procedures involve custom-made prosthetic devices that are cemented into place, patients frequently confuse the two. While they share similarities in materials and aesthetics, their primary purposes are entirely different. This guide will clarify the distinctions between dental crowns and bridges to help you better understand your treatment options.

## Understanding Dental Crowns

A dental crown is essentially a protective cap designed to cover a single existing tooth. It is used when a tooth is severely damaged but still has healthy roots that can be saved.

### What is a Dental Crown?

A crown encases the entire visible portion of a tooth that lies at and above the gumline. By covering the tooth completely, the crown restores its original shape, size, strength, and appearance. Crowns can be crafted from various materials, including porcelain, ceramic, zirconia, or metal alloys. Porcelain and ceramic are the most popular choices for visible teeth because they can be color-matched to your natural enamel.

### When is a Crown Recommended?

Dentists recommend crowns for a variety of structural and cosmetic reasons, including:

*   **Severe Decay:** When a cavity is too large for a standard filling, a crown is needed to support the remaining tooth structure.
*   **Cracked or Broken Teeth:** A crown holds together parts of a cracked tooth and protects it from breaking further under chewing pressure.
*   **Post-Root Canal Protection:** After a root canal, the tooth becomes brittle. A crown is placed over it to provide essential strength.
*   **Cosmetic Enhancement:** Crowns can cover severely discolored, misshapen, or unusually small teeth to improve the overall smile.

### The Crown Placement Process

Getting a crown typically requires two visits. During the first visit, the dentist numbs the tooth and carefully files away a thin layer of enamel to make room for the crown. An impression of the prepared tooth is taken and sent to a dental laboratory where the custom crown is fabricated. The dentist places a temporary crown to protect the tooth in the meantime. During the second visit, the temporary crown is removed, and the permanent custom crown is checked for fit and securely cemented into place.

## Understanding Dental Bridges

While a crown repairs a damaged tooth, a dental bridge is designed to replace one or more completely missing teeth.

### What is a Dental Bridge?

A bridge physically "bridges the gap" left by missing teeth. A traditional bridge consists of one or more artificial teeth (called pontics) held in place by dental crowns attached to the natural teeth adjacent to the empty space. These adjacent teeth are known as abutment teeth. Like crowns, bridges are typically made from porcelain or ceramic fused to a strong underlying framework.

### When is a Bridge Recommended?

A bridge is recommended when a patient has lost a tooth due to extraction, severe decay, or trauma. Replacing a missing tooth is critical not just for aesthetics, but also for oral health. If a gap is left empty, the surrounding teeth will gradually shift out of position, leading to bite misalignment, jaw pain, and an increased risk of gum disease.

### The Bridge Placement Process

The procedure for a bridge is very similar to getting a crown, but it involves multiple teeth. The dentist prepares the abutment teeth on either side of the gap by filing them down. Impressions are taken of the prepared teeth and the gap, which serve as the blueprint for the lab to create the bridge. A temporary bridge is placed to protect the exposed teeth. Once the permanent bridge is ready, it is meticulously adjusted for proper bite alignment and cemented onto the abutment teeth.

## Key Differences Between Crowns and Bridges

To summarize, here are the primary distinctions between the two restorative treatments:

### Purpose
*   **Crown:** Repairs, strengthens, and protects a single damaged tooth.
*   **Bridge:** Replaces one or more missing teeth by filling the empty space.

### Number of Teeth Involved
*   **Crown:** Involves only the specific tooth that is damaged.
*   **Bridge:** Involves at least three teeth—the missing tooth (pontic) and the two healthy adjacent teeth (abutments) that anchor the prosthetic.

### Support Structure
*   **Crown:** Rests securely on the natural root of the damaged tooth.
*   **Bridge:** Relies entirely on the strength of the adjacent healthy teeth for support.

## Which Restoration is Right for You?

The choice between a crown and a bridge is usually dictated by your specific dental situation rather than personal preference. 

If your tooth is heavily decayed but the root is intact, a crown is the appropriate choice to save the tooth. Dentists always prefer to preserve natural tooth structure whenever possible. 

If the tooth cannot be saved and must be extracted, or if it is already missing, a bridge is an excellent option to restore your chewing ability and prevent your remaining teeth from shifting. (It is worth noting that a dental implant is another highly effective alternative to a bridge for replacing missing teeth, as it does not require altering the adjacent healthy teeth.)

## Caring for Your Dental Restorations

Both crowns and bridges require diligent oral hygiene to ensure their longevity. While the ceramic material cannot decay, the natural tooth structure underneath the crown or the abutment teeth supporting the bridge are still vulnerable to cavities and gum disease.

You should brush twice daily and floss carefully. For bridges, special floss threaders or water flossers are highly recommended to clean the space underneath the artificial pontic, where plaque and food debris tend to accumulate.

## Frequently Asked Questions (FAQs)

**How long do crowns and bridges last?**
With proper oral hygiene and regular dental check-ups, high-quality crowns and bridges can last anywhere from 10 to 15 years, and in many cases, even longer.

**Does getting a crown or bridge hurt?**
No, the procedures are performed under local anesthesia. You will not feel any pain while the teeth are being prepared. You may experience mild sensitivity for a few days after the procedure.

**Can a bridge be removed for cleaning?**
No, a traditional dental bridge is permanently cemented onto the abutment teeth and can only be removed by a dentist. (Removable partial dentures are a different type of prosthetic.)

**What happens if the teeth supporting my bridge decay?**
If an abutment tooth develops severe decay, the entire bridge may fail. This is why meticulous daily cleaning around and underneath the bridge is absolutely critical.
